# AGENTS.md

> **Read [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) § "Anti-Slop Design Law" before any design or frontend work, and [ANTI-SLOP.md](ANTI-SLOP.md) for the prose source of truth.** These are Dr Non's hard rules against AI design slop: empty space is a decision, subtract before you add, the map comes first, and the page is a sequence of attention ordered by importance — not by habit.

They are **machine-enforced**, not a matter of good intentions. `npm run verify:release` fails the build on a violation, and the gate list is in CLAUDE.md. **If a gate fires, fix the design — do not edit the gate.** A gate that gets edited to go green is worse than no gate: it teaches people to route around it.

**Spatial hallucination is also slop.** An interface that invents a reason for things to occupy space — instead of arranging them around how people read, choose and act — fails the same test, whether or not it looks composed. Ask of every element: *why is this here, why this size, and why this far from that?* "The grid put it there" is not an answer. `npm run check:spatial` measures this in a real browser across 15 routes × the dense ladder (every 20px from 320 to 1920): dead bands, orphan rows, stranded items, accidental wrapping, clipped text, scattered actions, false hierarchy, and the cost of the first decision. **A widow is a hole, not a count** — a lone item on the last row is a defect only when it leaves empty space beside it; a row that grew to fill its line has no hole and is not a defect. **A grid that strands one item is the same hole drawn in cells**: the last row of a grid must not hold a single item, whatever the item is — a chip, a card, a rule. Fix it by choosing a column count that divides the item count, or by the coda (`.coda-grid`, `.coda-grid-3`), which lets the last card take the full width so the row is full. The gate fails on **any** defect; the baseline only records the wraps a phone genuinely forced. See CLAUDE.md § "Spatial hallucination".

**A page needs one grid, and every band on it has to be on that grid.** The homepage already had one — six columns, 2rem gutters — and three bands ignored it: the thesis and verdict were centred inside the section, and the Alpha header ran its own `1.45fr/1fr` ratio. One screen ended up carrying **four unrelated left edges — 25, 385, 410, 865** — with the right third of two bands empty and nothing for the eye to navigate by. Every band now sits on the same six columns, lead on the left axis and support on the right, so the page has one spine from the thesis down to the tier cards. **A band that starts where no other band starts has invented its own grid.** The note must not precede the thing it notes: the Alpha headline now comes before the amber panel it belongs with.

**The first line has to clear the band.** The masthead is fixed, so the page scrolls under it; the first thing a reader sees must start at least 32px below its rule, or the page reads as pasted under a lid. `npm run check:clearance` holds that across 15 routes × 4 widths.

**An index that answers with citations has to describe the bytes it was built from.** The knowledge rack is the site's question box over the methodology, and it cites the documents it was built from. `npm run check:knowledge-rack` proves that every hash in the manifest matches the source on disk, every source is indexed, and the published catalog is the same bytes as the built one. `npm run check:contacts` keeps personal contact details out of the corpus entirely. To change the corpus, drop the document into `knowledge-rack/inbox/` and run `python3 scripts/build_knowledge_rack.py` — never hand-edit the manifest. See CLAUDE.md § "The knowledge rack".

**The author's name is one fact: `Dr Non Arkaraprasertkul` / `ดร.นน อัครประเสริฐกุล`.** The site had been publishing three different renderings, across the footer, the awards page, the history timeline, the citation guidance, the CSV and the PDF metadata. `npm run check:author-name` fails the build if any locale drops the full surname or if a wrong form reappears anywhere in the tree. The short form "Dr. Non" / "ดร.นน" is fine on its own; a surname, when it appears, is the full one. See CLAUDE.md § "The author's name".

**The brand mark is artwork, and it is derived.** The footer used to carry a black square with "SL" set in it — a placeholder for a mark the site did not have. It now carries the real SLIC Index wordmark, keyed off its paper so it sits on the cream with no plate of its own. The masthead carries the same keyed file, because the bar is one opaque cream band and a cut-out edge on a transparent plate was the thing that made the mark look pasted on. One mark, two surfaces, one rule, and both are gated. `public/Logos/slic-index-mark.png` is **generated** — do not hand-edit it. Run `python3 scripts/build-brand-mark.py`; it writes the PNG and `scripts/brand-mark.provenance.json`, and the integrity gate checks the PNG's transparency, its match to the built copy, and both SHA-256 hashes. See CLAUDE.md § "Branding".

**The supporter row is one line, and every mark in it is keyed.** The footer carries six marks — RCAD (รวพ.), depa, Smart City Thailand Office, ReTL, Axiom and SLIC — in a single line with no boxes, no per-logo frames, and no white plates. UWN (บพท.) is out: it is a unit inside RCAD now, and the roster names the agency. The marks are generated: sources in `logo-sources/logos/`, transparent PNGs in `public/Logos/`, produced by `scripts/build-partner-logos.sh`. A mark that still carries its paper shows a rectangle on the cream. The row must never wrap. All of it is gated. See CLAUDE.md § "Branding".

**The masthead is one ground, and the controls in it are bare marks.** The bar used to be a translucent dark plate inset inside the viewport, and inside it a search button with its own plate, a ⌘K keycap with a box, a native select with the browser's white card glued to it, and a burger with a border — five nested treatments, the Rams rule as a box inside a box inside a box. Now the band is opaque cream, full bleed, with a hairline rule at the bottom, and every control in it is a bare mark on that ground. Nothing in the bar paints a background, carries a border, or floats on a shadow. `npm run check:masthead` fails the build otherwise. See CLAUDE.md § "Key Rules".

**The footer's navigation is a sitemap, and a sitemap is packed.** On a phone it used to be a two-column grid in which every row took the height of its tallest cell: the 44px "Home" link sat alone in a 211px row with 167px of nothing under it, and the gap between "Data" and "About" was 190px. It also printed "Rankings" and "Methodology" twice — once as a heading, once as that heading's own first link. The way it is packed now is structural: a group's links run on **one line**, separated by a middot, so every block in the sitemap is about the same height, and equal blocks cannot leave a hole. A heading that repeats one of its links *becomes* that link, so the word appears once and no page is lost. `npm run check:footer-nav` fails the build on a hole over 36px — under a block of a row, or under a column that stops short of the bottom — a repeated heading, a route that is no longer one click away, a wrapped label, or a navigation block taller than one screen. See CLAUDE.md § "Key Rules".

**The reader pays for the pixels they can see.** A page can pass every design gate and still cost 2MB before it is useful. `npm run check:payload` measures what a reader actually pays: no image may be painted into a box from a file more than three times wider than that box, no route's first load may exceed 1,200kB, the shell may not preload a chunk over 300kB, and the font stylesheet must carry the reader's own script — the three CJK families are 1.29MB of rules, and an English reader was paying for all of them. The photographs are served as the picture the page can show (`scripts/generate-thumbnails.mjs` writes them; the original stays for the places that show a photograph large), the fonts are requested per locale before the first paint, and the 163-city dataset is reached by the routes that need it rather than by the masthead that prints its count. A corpus is fetched when the reader asks a question of it, not when they arrive at the page that mentions it. See CLAUDE.md § "The reader pays for the pixels they can see".
